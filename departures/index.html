    Secret controls

    • Tap rows (board by destination): each row is a different first letter. Tap the row for each of the first three letters of their destination. See “Tapping rows” below.
    • Tap letters (board by time): tap the first three letters of their destination, wherever you can see them on the board. After each tap, “Departures” changes for 2 seconds to the place it matches so far. Tap a number in the time or platform column, before or after the letters, to choose the platform.
    • Hold the station name (1 second): opens what looks like a station search. Type the spectator's destination and press Go. The board doesn't change yet.
    • Double-tap the clock: reveal now.
    • Double-tap the station name: reset the board, ready for the next performance. After a reveal you must reset before tapping rows again.
    • Hold the clock (1 second): open this screen.

    What to type in the search

    • whitby  destination only. Platform and time are made up.
    • whitby 7  platform 7.
    • whitby 7 1947  platform 7, leaves at 19:47.
    • whitby 7 1947 / sarah  adds “Seat reserved for SARAH” to the scrolling strip.

    Any place name works, not only ones in the list. Two-digit numbers are the platform, three or four digits are the time.

    Tapping rows

    • The board opens as “Departures by destination”: one train for each letter, in alphabetical order. It scrolls, like a real one.
    • A tap anywhere on a row counts as the first letter of that row's station, whatever part of the row you touch. You don't tap letters inside a name. For York, find the station starting with Y and tap it, then the station starting with O, then the one starting with R: “You could have gone here, or here, or here…”
    • Scroll the board to reach rows further down. A scroll never counts as a tap.
    • No station starts with X. For a letter that has no row, tap the column headings (Time, Destination, Plat) instead. It counts as “any letter”: E, headings, E finds Exeter.
    • Take as long as you like between taps, and scroll as much as you need. After every tap, “Departures” shows the place it matches so far, for 2 seconds. Keep tapping until it shows the right one. If nothing matches, it shows the letters it received instead.
    • The letters are forgotten 20 seconds after your last tap, at the reveal, and when you double-tap the station name. Made a mistake? Double-tap the station name and start again.
    • The reveal delay counts from your last tap.
    • At the reveal the board scrolls to the top and rattles round into a normal departures board, with their train first.
    • After the reveal the board stays in time order. Double-tap the station name to bring back the board by destination.

    Tapping letters

    • Say they chose York. Point at the Y in one station, the O in another and the R in a third: “You could have chosen this one, or this one, or this one…”
    • The letters can come from any station, in any row, in any order on the screen. Only the order you tap them matters.
    • Three letters is usually enough. Where several places share three letters, it picks the biggest: BRI gives Bristol, so tap BRIG for Brighton. Keep tapping more letters if you need to. Check the name it shows.
    • Every letter except J is always somewhere on the board. If you can't find one, or the place isn't found, use the station search instead.
    • The board won't let a train depart while you're tapping, so the letters don't move under your finger.
    • A tap on a blank flap starts again.

    How to perform

    1. Prop the phone on the table, board facing everyone. Landscape looks best. Let it run: trains leave and new ones appear on their own.
    2. Ask for any place in the country, a number for the platform, and a time. Get the information however suits you: they tell you, or you peek a written answer.
    3. Point at stations on the board while you talk and tap their letters (see above). Or pick up the phone “to check the trains”, hold the station name and type the details into the search.
    4. After your chosen delay, the next train departs, the whole board rattles round, and their train is at the top, with the announcement.
